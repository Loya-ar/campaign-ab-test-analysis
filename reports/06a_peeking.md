# Step 6a: the cost of peeking (A/A simulation)

Generated by `python scripts/peeking_simulation.py`.

Each simulated test takes the 21,306 real **no-email** customers, shuffles them and splits them into two arms of 10,653. Nobody got an email, so any 'significant' difference is a false positive. An analyst checks the z-test at equally spaced points and stops at the first p < 0.05. 2,000 simulations per row, seed 42. Monte Carlo standard error at 5% is about 0.5%.

With O'Brien-Fleming boundaries the critical value is very strict early and close to 1.96 at the end; the constant is calibrated so the overall false-positive rate is 5%.

**Reading it:** one look gives about the promised 5%. Checking the same test repeatedly and stopping at the first significant result multiplies the false-positive rate, even though the data and the test are exactly the same. A pre-set group-sequential boundary (or simply fixing the sample size in advance and looking once) brings it back to about 5%.

The single-look rates differ from 5% by no more than about 2 Monte Carlo standard errors. Conversion has only about 61 buyers per arm here, so its z-test rests on a rougher normal approximation than visit (about 1,130 visitors per arm). The 5-look OBF constant (2.04 at the last look) matches the published O'Brien-Fleming value.
